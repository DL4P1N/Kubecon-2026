Why Your Monitoring Stack is a Security Risk
For years, the Kubernetes community has lived with a “necessary evil”: granting monitoring agents and health checkers the nodes/proxy permission. While intended for simple metrics scraping, this permission is effectively a node-level superuser capability.
With the graduation of Fine-Grained Kubelet API Authorization to General Availability in v1.36, Kubernetes finally provides a robust, least-privilege alternative.
Speakers: Vinayak Goyal, Graham Helton. Track: Security.

Questions

Do I need to give my email?

No. You need a name and one topic. Email is optional and not stored.

Does the app track where I am?

No. Check-in is a QR scan at the door. There is no location tracking.

What happens to my data after the event?

Your profile stays for your next conference until you delete it.

How do organizers use it for future talks?

They see topic counts, never individuals, and use them to choose talks.

Is the cluster map proven?

No. It is untested on live data and can look sparse at 200 attendees.
